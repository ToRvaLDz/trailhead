# Grilling
Interrogate the human live, one question at a time, until the decision is unambiguous and its rationale is captured. This is human-in-the-loop: the decision is theirs, so never answer on their behalf, never assume an answer you haven't heard, never proceed on silence.

- **Map the decision as a tree.** Every choice branches into the choices that hang off it. The *frontier* is every question whose prerequisites are already settled: the ones you can ask now without guessing. A question whose answer depends on another still-open one isn't on the frontier yet; it arrives there the moment that answer lands. You don't ask the frontier in a batch: you pick one from it, and recompute after the answer.
- **Breadth before depth: enumerate the whole surface first.** When the subject is a space with many parts (mapping an app or a project's scope, not a single isolated choice), open by enumerating every functional area it implies (for a video player: media library, search, thumbnails, filters, genres, the player itself, ...) and get them all onto the record before deep-diving any one. Depth on the current thread is seductive, but the tree only ever grows the questions you actually raise, so an area you never name never enters it and stays silently missing. Fan out first (name every area), then converge each. The deliverable is coverage of the whole surface, not a deep answer on one corner of it.
- **One question at a time, with your pick.** Number the question, state it, and offer your recommended answer, stress-tested per the next bullet. Then stop and wait. Each answer reshapes the tree: settled decisions push the frontier outward. Recompute and ask the next. Grilling stays human-decides: the **grilled questions themselves never offer the delegate option** (a recommended answer is not the delegate option), because converging a decision is the human's. A separate meta-ask a call-site may put *before* grilling (e.g. the `build`/`quick` Discuss "do you want a grilling round?") is its own advisory choice and can offer delegate/defer per that call-site. **When a grilled decision is genuinely blocked on a prerequisite, offer defer in prose** (state it in words as part of the question), per the recipe in `../choices.md`.
- **Stress-test your pick before offering it**, per **Stress-test the pick before offering it** in `../choices.md` (one pass, its `Weak point:` line shown with the pick). In grilling, its fit check names the earlier questions it touches by question number; it stays a local check, since the whole set is weighed later, in the holistic review below.
- **Facts are your job, decisions are theirs.** When a question needs a fact from the environment (filesystem, code, tools), go find it yourself; never ask the human what you could look up. Only questions downstream of that lookup wait; ask the rest now.
- **Refuse vague and hedged answers.** "Good," "fast," "simple," "users," "later" are not answers: probe. *Fast how: sub-second, or handles large datasets? Users: the Customer or the operator?* Make the abstract concrete: "walk me through it," "give me an example," "what does that actually look like?"
- **Offer concrete options to react to, and show them, not just name them.** Present 2–4 sharp interpretations or specific choices that reveal priorities: never generic buckets ("technical/business/other") or leading options. **Naming the options is not enough: illustrate each one before asking the human to choose**, so the alternatives are legible without the user having to ask. For each option say what it is, what it optimizes for, and what it sacrifices; for a choice spanning several dimensions, lay the options out side by side (a comparison table or widget) as the natural default, rather than listing A/B/C inline in the question and waiting for the user to request the detail. But when the human signals they want to explain in their own words, drop the options and let them; resume structured questions after.
- **Surface hidden assumptions.** Say the unstated premise out loud and put it to them: "this only works if X is true, is it?" Cross-check claims against the code; when they contradict, name the contradiction and force a choice.
- **Steelman and play devil's advocate.** State the strongest case for the option they're leaning away from. Attack the option they favor from its weakest seam. If it survives, the decision is stronger; if it cracks, you found the real question.
- **Detect and open up tradeoffs.** When "or / versus / torn between / which is better" appears, pause: name what each side optimizes for, what each sacrifices, which aligns with the goal, then recommend with reasoning. Skip for trivial or already-decided choices.
- **Play back your understanding** before closing each branch: restate the decision and its rationale in your words and have them confirm or correct.
- **Stop only when the frontier is empty**: every branch visited, nothing silently assumed, the choice unambiguous *and* the why recorded. **For a multi-part space** (per the breadth bullet above) also require every functional area to have been enumerated and visited: an empty frontier reached while whole areas were never raised is a false finish, because the tree only ever grew the questions you raised. A single isolated choice has no areas to enumerate, so its empty frontier is a real finish. The empty frontier is then followed by the **Holistic review before the final confirmation** below: the final set is not confirmed or recorded before it has run.
- **Holistic review before the final confirmation.** Once the frontier is empty and before the final confirmation, re-read all settled choices as a whole, against each other and against the destination (or the ticket's goal), because answers taken one at a time can fit badly together. Report (a) the proposed amendments: what you would change in the set where choices do not fit together; and (b) the non-working combinations: contradictions, gaps, choices that break each other or the destination. Each item names the choice(s) it touches by question number. **Every non-working combination carries its fix**, stress-tested like any pick: the choice to change and to what, the question to reopen, or the ticket that will carry it. Only when no fix exists inside the set (it needs a fact not yet known, or it lies beyond the destination) say why in one line and name where it goes (a `research` ticket, a seed, the ticket that already tracks it). Accepting a combination as a risk is never the proposed fix and never a plain pointer to another ticket: it is the human's call, made on the menu below. **Nothing found:** say so in one line and go straight to the confirmation. **Otherwise** ask the human to apply, amend or keep: apply (the first option, recommended) applies every proposed amendment and fix; amend lets them rewrite an item or pick among them (mixed per-item answers go here); keep leaves the choices as they were. Whatever the answer, every non-working combination still standing afterwards (its fix not applied, or kept) is recorded with the set as a consciously accepted risk, never silently dropped. An applied amendment or fix is re-confirmed in one play-back; it reopens that branch (the grill resumes from there) only when it undoes the rationale the branch was settled on, and then the review runs again on the new set, at most once more: whatever that second review still finds is put to the human the same way, and anything left standing is recorded as accepted risk, with no third pass. Only then is the final set played back, confirmed and recorded. **Format:** a forced choice of 3 real options, so an `AskUserQuestion` menu per the "Which format" rule in `../choices.md` (a numbered list on a host without one), without the delegate option (the set is the human's) and without defer; the grilled questions themselves stay conversational prose. A single isolated choice still runs it (usually the one-line nothing found).
