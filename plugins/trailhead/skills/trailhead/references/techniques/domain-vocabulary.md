# Domain vocabulary
Build a shared, precise vocabulary as you design, so each key term means exactly one thing. Active work — challenge terms, invent edge cases, write definitions down the moment they crystallize — not passive reading.

- **Define each key term once, canonically.** One name, one meaning. Capture it the instant it's resolved; don't batch for later.
- **Hunt the vocabulary smells.** *Synonyms* — two words for one concept (collapse them). *Homonyms* — one word for two concepts (split them, name each). *Overloaded words* — "account," "user," "cancel" carrying several meanings at once. When fuzzy, propose a precise replacement: "you said 'account' — the Customer or the login User? Those are different things."
- **A fuzzy term usually hides a real decision.** When people can't pin a word down, an unmade choice is lurking under it. Surface the decision and settle it, then name the result — don't paper over it with a definition.
- **Stress-test with concrete scenarios.** Invent edge cases that probe the boundary between two concepts and force precision about where one ends and the other begins.
- **Reuse the settled words everywhere** — questions, options, playbacks, and the written record all speak the same vocabulary. This ubiquitous language is what stops silent drift.
- **The glossary lives in the map's `## Notes`** — a glossary and nothing else, free of implementation detail. It evolves continuously: every term you sharpen lands there, always current and one lookup away.
